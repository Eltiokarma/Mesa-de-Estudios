# t172: The Frog That Breathes Through Its Skin

(1) Lake Titicaca, which lies between Peru and Bolivia, is home to a frog that spends its whole life under water: the Titicaca water frog. (2) It is one of the largest water frogs in the world, and its loose, wrinkled skin looks too big for its body. (3) These folds are not a mistake of nature. (4) Like all amphibians, this frog can take in oxygen through its skin, and the folds increase the surface that touches the water.

(5) The lake is at about 3,800 meters above sea level, where the water holds little oxygen, so every extra centimeter of skin helps. (6) Researchers have also seen the frogs move up and down on the lake bottom, like a person doing push-ups, which probably brings fresher water to their skin.

(7) Their special body, however, does not protect them from people. (8) Pollution from cities and mines makes the water dirtier, and introduced fish, such as trout, can eat young frogs. (9) In addition, many frogs have been captured to be sold as food. (10) The species is now considered critically endangered, so protecting the lake's water may be the best way to protect the frog.

# t173: Give the Weekends Back

(1) Every Friday afternoon, the same scene repeats in many schools: teachers hand out worksheets, and students sigh. (2) I have been a teacher for many years, and I used to believe that weekend homework made students responsible. (3) Now I am not so sure.

(4) Students need time to rest, to play sports, to help at home and to be with their families. (5) When two full days are filled with assignments, those activities become a burden, and tired students return on Monday with less energy. (6) Some people argue that, without weekend homework, students will forget what they learned. (7) That is a fair worry, but it can be solved with short exercises during the week and good planning by teachers.

(8) A ban would also oblige schools to think about quality: a few well-chosen tasks are worth more than many boring ones. (9) For these reasons, schools should stop giving homework on weekends. (10) Rest is not the opposite of learning; it is part of it.
